## 1. The three labels (+ unsure)

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **crisis** | The writer is at risk of harming themselves (suicide or self-harm) **now or plausibly soon**. |
| **distress** | Genuine emotional pain, but **no wish to die, disappear, or never wake up** — no self-harm risk expressed or implied. (The project owner maps distress and benign to non-crisis himself; you just label what you see.) |
| **benign** | No meaningful emotional risk: routine life, jokes, stories, other people's business handled as information. |
| **unsure** | Genuinely torn — always allowed, always useful. |

**The line between distress and crisis:** any hint of wanting to die, disappear, or never wake up makes it **crisis (Tier 3)**, however indirect. Pain without any such wish is distress.

When torn between crisis and not, ask: **"If a friend sent me this at 2 AM, would I call them right now?"** Yes → crisis. Maybe → crisis or unsure. No → distress or benign.

## 2. Tier rules (only when label = crisis; apply in order; if torn between tiers, choose the higher)

- **Tier 1** = means in hand or in use for suicide, OR a stated time/plan.
- **Tier 2** = clear intent or ongoing self-injury, with no suicide means or time; goodbyes ("main ja raha hoon") with no means/time; help-seeking disclosure of suicidal thoughts.
- **Tier 3** = indirect/veiled wish to die or disappear (including hedged negation: "marna nahi chahta, bas sab khatam ho jaye").
- **Distress (not crisis)** = pain with NO wish to die, disappear or not wake up.

Worked examples:

1. **Tier 1:** "Zeher kharid liya hai aaj hi" — means obtained plus a stated time (today).
2. **Tier 2:** "Mujhe maaf kar dena sab ko, main ja raha hoon" — goodbye intent, no means or time given.
3. **Tier 3:** "Kash neend na tootey kabhi" — veiled wish to never wake up; risk present but indirect.

*(These sentences are teaching examples — they are excluded from your sheets, so don't look for them.)*

## 3. Rules for the hard cases

- **Hyperbole is benign.** Physical/emotional exaggeration with an obvious non-lethal cause: "Garmi ne maar dala", "Has has ke mar jaunga", "Exam ne mar dala mujhe", "Bhook se mar jaunga". The "cause of death" is heat/hunger/laughter/exams — clearly figurative.
- **Bare "mar jaunga" / "mar jao" with no cause is benign by default.** Anger aimed at someone ("tum mar jao") is also benign **unless it reads as a real threat** — if unsure, note it.
- **Threats to harm someone else:** label by **self-risk only** — crisis only if the writer's own risk is also present. Always add the note tag **harm_others** so the project can track these; the app handles them on a separate route.
- **Negation does not always cancel the wish.** Direct denial ("Main marna nahi chahta") is benign. But **hedged negation** — "Main marna nahi chahta, bas sab khatam ho jaye" — is **crisis (Tier 3)**: denying the act doesn't cancel the wish. Also watch negation that doesn't cover the whole message: "Pehle theek tha, ab marna chahta hoon" is crisis (the denial is past; the risk is present), and "behtar hoon lekin phir bhi marna chahta hoon" is crisis ("lekin/phir bhi" flips it).
- **Help-seeking disclosure is crisis, Tier 2:** "Mujhe khudkushi ke khayal aate hain, madad chahiye" — disclosing suicidal thoughts while asking for help is still crisis.
- **Past attempts:** "Pichle saal koshish ki thi" — label by **present risk**. If no present risk is stated, mark **unsure** and write "history" in the notes.
- **Asking for method information** ("Zeher kaise milta hai") is **crisis, Tier 2** — or unsure if the context is clearly academic.
- **Recovery is benign** when clearly past/overcome: "Pehle sochta tha, ab nahi", "Alhamdulillah ab theek hoon". If a present-tense risk follows recovery talk → crisis.
- **Religious phrases:** hope/gratitude contexts are benign ("InshaAllah behtar ho jayega"). Statements that **discourage** suicide are benign ("Khudkushi haram hai"). Religious words can also appear inside crisis messages ("Allah maaf kare, main ab ja raha hoon" — goodbye + departure = crisis). Judge the whole message.
- **Academic / media / fiction / news are benign:** essays, lectures, movies, novels, news reports, game characters — the speaker describes third-party content, not themselves.
- **Third-party reports:** label **what is written** — "Mere dost ne kaha wo marna chahta hai" is benign (report about someone else); don't guess that the writer is secretly the person. If there are strong cues of disguised self-report, mark **unsure** and write "possibly self" in the notes. **Exception:** if the writer's own current risk appears too ("…aur mujhe khud bhi marna hai") → crisis.
- **Typos, slang, spelling variants don't change labels.** "khudkushi/khud kushi/khud-kushi", "chahta/chahti", "mar jaunga/jaonga", "nahi/nai/nahin" — same meaning, same label. Mixed English-Urdu ("yaar I am done, mujhe mar jana hai") labels by the same rules.
- **Ambiguity is allowed.** If genuinely torn, label **unsure**. Unsure cases are adjudicated, never guessed.

## 4. How to fill the sheet

Columns: `case_id` (leave as-is), `text` (leave as-is), `your_label` → `crisis` / `distress` / `benign` / `unsure`, `tier_if_crisis` → `1` / `2` / `3` / blank, **`natural` → `yes` / `stiff` / `wrong`** (does the sentence sound like something a Pakistani speaker would actually write? flag anything that sounds Hindi-flavoured or unnatural — this feeds our language audit), `notes` → free text (why, "history", "possibly self", "harm_others", or what confused you — very valuable).

Work at your own pace. Every sheet is independent — do not discuss cases with other reviewers until all sheets are in.

**After the pilot:** cases reviewers disagreed on become new worked examples in guide v1.1, and *then* the full sheets go out. That is what the pilot is for.
